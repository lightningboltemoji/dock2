# The Finder tile's own icon

> Change `1792815242` — 2026-10-23

## Goal

A retro look for Files: the Finder tile can wear one of Cairo's pictures, white on any colour the user
picks, as a macOS 27 icon of Liquid Glass. Two things had to hold together: Icon Composer's own look, and
the pictures' pixels kept crisp at every tile size.

## Implementation

**The pictures.** 59 of Cairo's pictures, chosen by eye and named by hand, are in `CairoPicture.swift`. Each
is cropped to its ink as rows of `#` and `.`, and they are grouped by kind (flowers, trees, fruit,
animals…), which is the picker's order. A picture is stored by its case name: Cairo's character for it means
nothing, and an index would break on a reorder.

**Why the plate and the picture have different owners.** `ictool`, Icon Composer's renderer, is in Xcode
alone, so it can run only at build time. Whole icons rendered ahead were rejected. The tile's size is any
value from 16 to 128 pt, and even a render snapped to 48 pt is soft, since the glass darkens each edge of the
picture by about a pixel. Up close, the glass does next to nothing to one-pixel lines: at 128 pt a glass
picture, an opaque glass one and flat white look alike. So dock2 draws the picture itself and loses little.
Drawing the plate ourselves, or driving the private `IconRendering.framework`, were not needed.

**The plate in any colour.** A first test said a blend of two references could not reproduce Icon
Composer's plate. It was wrong. `ictool` writes Display P3, so the requested orange `(230, 115, 38)` came
back as `(215, 121, 59)`, which is the same colour in P3, and the test had mixed sRGB inputs with P3
outputs. Redone in P3, `black + (white − black) × colour` per channel matches over six colours never
rendered, at about a level in 255 on average. The exception is the rim, the outer 1.5%, where Icon Composer
lightens in the colour's own hue: there the blend averages 13 levels off. A third reference, grey, with a
quadratic only brings that to 8, because the rim mixes channels. The simple blend was kept. `ictool` tags a
render holding only neutrals sRGB, and the white one P3, so the profile has to come from the white one: a
compositing pass that took it from the black one washed every colour out. Dark, Clear (light and dark) and
Tinted plates are pixel-identical between an orange and a green fill, which leaves one reference each for
later.

**The picture.** Each of its pixels covers `round(0.62 × plate / longer side)` of the screen's, its corner on
a whole pixel (`PixelArt.placement`). With less than 3/4 of a screen pixel apiece, the widest pictures on the
smallest tiles, it scales down smoothly rather than outgrow the plate. Its shadow — 35% black, blurred 2% of
the plate, 0.8% down — was fitted by eye against `ictool`'s. The plate is 412/512 of the image, as measured
on `NSWorkspace`'s Finder, Ghostty and Preview icons, all 206 of 256.

**Into the dock.** `FinderTileImage` returns an `NSImage` with a drawing handler, as `PluginTileImage` does.
`TileLayer` rasterises it at the tile's size and scale, so the handler learns the exact pixel side and
snaps there. `DockController` swaps it in for Finder's icon and rebuilds it when `Settings.finderIcon`
changes. `FinderIcon` holds an optional picture and a colour in Display P3, so going back to Finder's own
icon keeps the colour. The Settings picker is a grid drawn by the same images and a `ColorPicker`.

**Crisp in the dock, not only in the image.** A live capture at 128 pt had no in-between pixels at all where
ink met plate. Captures at 41.3 and 64 pt had a lot: 94% and 71% of the ink's count. There were two causes,
and both applied to every tile. `DockView` drew a tile's image again only when the image or the scale
changed, so a new tile size stretched the old rendering. `TileLayer` now draws again on a new size. And the
layout gives icons fractional origins and sizes — a slot at x = 640.725, a 41.3 pt tile 82.6 px wide — so the
bitmap was resampled. `DockView` now places icons with `pixelSnapped`, origin and size on whole device pixels,
which moves an icon by less than half a pixel. Captured again at 41.3, 47.14 and 64 pt, the tile had no
in-between pixels at any of them.

**Checked.** An offline harness built from the same sources drew the image as `TileLayer` does. At 128 pt it
was 4.4 levels from `ictool`'s own render on average, mostly at the picture's edges, which Icon Composer shades
and this draws flat. From 24 pt up each Cairo pixel stayed whole; at 16 pt the TV scales down. In the debug
dock at 128 pt, the tile went from Finder to the palm tree to the dog as the setting was written, with no
restart. The picker was rendered with `ImageRenderer` outside a `Form`, which it cannot draw.

## Observations

- Default style only. Where the system keeps its icon style is unread: only `AppleIconAppearanceTintColor`
  is in the global domain here. Dark's picture colour and shading are unfitted (`design/files.md`).
- The rim reads duller than Icon Composer's.
- The picker was never clicked in the real Settings window.
- Sharpness was measured live with a Cairo picture on Finder's tile only. App icons get the same snapping and
  redraw on resize, which was not looked at for them.
- The pictures' names are a first pass, among them `man` and `woman` for two profiles and `firecracker`.
