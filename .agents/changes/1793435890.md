# The sideline on springs

> Change `1793435890` — 2026-10-31

## Goal

Sidelining moved everything on the Dock's measured reshape, 360 ms on one ease-in-out curve, and filmed it read
as a slide rather than a move to the bench. The crossing tile slid flat along the baseline and under the tiles it
passed, since it sat lower in the layer order. The puck budded on the same curve as the rest. The sideline is
dock2's own, with no Dock motion to match, so it can move the way search's panel does: on springs, a little past
and back.

## Implementation

**`SidelineMotion`** (Core) holds two springs in `Spring`'s terms, response and fraction of critical, as
`GrowthMotion` does. What moves or grows takes `out` (0.5 s, 0.75: there in 0.29 s, 3% past at 0.38 s); what
shrinks takes `in` (0.4 s, critically damped). The split is the panel's: its edges going out overshoot and coming
in never do. Here it keeps a puck drawing back to its 2pt speck from overshooting into a negative size. A glass
shape takes one spring for all its keys, chosen by whether it grows both ways. Split by key, its centre
overshooting while its size settled would move its edges apart. `Spring.progress(at:)` is the closed-form step
response, under-, critically and overdamped, which the tests check against the equation stepped finely.

**The crossing tile hops.** It flies above the other tiles (`zPosition` 1) and lifts a launch's hop high
(`tile / 4`) on the way: `4p(1 − p)` in the move's own progress p, so its path is a parabola in space whatever
the spring, and it is down when it first reaches its cell. The track is sampled at 120 Hz from the closed form
and played as an additive keyframe animation on `position`, after the spring on it. That needs Core Animation's
spring to be Core's, so `CASpringAnimation(_:)` builds it from mass 1, stiffness `(2π / response)²` and damping
`4π ζ / response`, the equation `Spring.step` integrates, rather than from `perceptualDuration:bounce:`, whose
mapping is undocumented. On `transform.translation.y` the lift would have been multiplied by the scale animating
the tile's size, three times too high at the start of a crossing from 128.

**Motion gains springs.** `Motion` keeps its duration and curve and may carry springs, which
`Motion.animation(_:from:growing:)` uses for every explicit animation; implicit ones cannot spring and keep to
the curve. `Motion.sideline` is the reshape with the sideline's springs, its duration the slower spring's
settling (for Reduce Motion's crossfade and completion blocks). `GlassLayer.setShapes`, the plain path in
`GlassView` and `Motion.animate` all build through it, so nothing else changes for curve motions.
`DockController.relayout` picks the sideline's motion when the puck's apps differ from the last layout's on the
glass both before and after. That covers the menu, a hand edit, Keep in Dock on a sidelined app, and a sidelined
app launching or quitting, which buds and draws back the puck on the same springs. Switching to the shelf with
apps sidelined is not one, since the shelf's implicit animations would take the springs' duration on the curve.

**The view moves layers explicitly.** `DockView.show` placed everything inside one implicit transaction, which
cannot spring, so tiles, dots and separators now move by `move(_:to:motion:)`: from the presentation, model set
with actions off, an explicit animation per key, nothing when the target is where the layer already is. Curve
motions look as before, but a move that interrupts another now starts from where the layer shows rather than
from its last model value. Tiles appearing (pop) and disappearing (shrink) went explicit for the same reason.

**A tile's size changes by a scale.** Animating `bounds` left the badge, shade and progress bar where the final
size puts them: a badged tile flying to the puck carried its badge near its lower left, and one flying back
carried it off past its corner. Now the bounds take the new size at once, drawn for it, and `transform.scale`
animates from the old size's ratio, so the sublayers scale with the icon. The badge's floor makes it 16pt on a 40pt
puck icon and 50pt on a 128pt tile, 40% and 39%, so the scaled badge meets the real one within a point at both ends.
A tile shrinking would start as its small picture stretched three times over, so `TileLayer.drawnAt` keeps the
picture at the old size until the show's animations finish, then draws it at its own. The badge's text layer
got `contents: NSNull`: it is redrawn in the commit of the move, which has actions on, and cross-faded its old
text over the new, filmed as a large ghost "32" beside the badge. The icon's own picture, redrawn for
`drawnAt`, is redrawn with actions off for the same reason.

A tile's dot stays where it stood when its tile goes to the puck, fading out. The code had placed it at the empty
rect a puck slot gives, so it slid toward the stage's origin as it faded (read from the code, not filmed). A dot
that is hidden goes to its new place unseen.

Rejected: baking the dock's motion from simulated springs into keyframes, as `FormDriver` does the panel's.
It would carry velocity through a change mid-flight, but every dock motion would need a driver holding each
layer's spring state, beside the hop, the entrance and auto-hide's slide, which are Core Animation's. A
sideline change mid-flight is rare (the menu takes longer than the motion), and from-presentation is what the
reshape already did. Also rejected: an overshooting Bézier for the curve, which gets one overshoot and no
settle.

**Validation**, on the debug build with its own config, tile 128, over a flat backdrop window at `.floating`,
recorded at 60 fps:

- Calculator from mid-row and a badged test app from the row's end, each to the puck and back. Both hop over the
  tiles between and land in their cells. The puck buds and reflows from one row to two.
- The badge rides its tile's top right and never ghosts.
- A per-frame score against neighbours shows each crossing as one smooth rise and fall, about 0.3 s of large
  motion, with no single-frame spike.
- Unsidelined and residelined 0.15 s apart: the tile turns back mid-air from where it showed and drops into
  the puck.
- A sidelined app quitting draws the puck back in. Relaunched, it buds the puck again, the tile growing in on
  the outward spring.
- Unchanged and clean: a running app quitting and launching, a tile dragged into the pinned apps (the gap
  opening), the tile size changed, Reduce Motion's crossfade, and Increase Contrast.
- `SidelineMotionTests` covers the closed form against the stepped equation at four dampings, both springs at
  rest and settling, `out` passing by 1–8% and `in` never, and the lift being one hop that ends at first
  arrival.

## Observations

- The springs are tuned by eye on one take at tile 128. The flight is quick across a long row (0.29 s whatever
  the distance, as springs are), and a slower `out` or a stagger, the row closing after the tile lifts, is
  untried.
- Under Increase Contrast the border's puck grows where it ends rather than budding with the glass: the
  union's path goes from one subpath to two, which Core Animation cannot interpolate. That predates this change.
- A change mid-flight restarts its springs from rest at the presentation, as the curve did. Additive springs
  would carry velocity through, but would need every animation on those keys to be additive.
- A tile that crossed keeps `zPosition` 1 until it next moves, which is harmless while nothing overlaps it.
- Not seen: the hop of a sidelined app launching (the test apps finish at once), the crossing at sizes other
  than 128, or the dark appearance.
