# The label follows the pointer, and snaps

> Change `1792552927` — 2026-10-20

## Goal

Moving quickly between two tiles showed the wrong name over the tile the pointer had reached, then the right
one: the dock read as laggy. It was suspected to be the single `LabelView` moving between tiles.

## Implementation

**Measured first**, at 120 frames a second with ScreenCaptureKit, the pointer driven from inside the recorder
(single jumps, and glides of a move every 8 ms, as a mouse reports them), with timestamped diagnostics in the
app. Moving the one view was not the fault: the real `LabelView`, compiled into a harness and driven by
`show` alone, changed name, place and glass in a single frame every time, full-screen over a platter glass
or not. Two faults lay around it.

- *The name crossfaded.* About 29 ms after reaching Ghostty the bubble stood over it, Ghostty's width,
  reading "Fiber", and the text faded to "Ghostty" over the next ~80 ms. The text layer carried a 0.25 s
  `contents` `CABasicAnimation` after every `show`: a `CATextLayer` draws its new string when the run loop's
  transaction commits, after `show`'s own no-actions transaction has closed, so the default action applies.
  The text layer now has no `contents` action, the idiom `DockView` already uses for its row's `sublayers`.
  The harness never showed the fade; the transaction its `show` ran in evidently differed, and it was not
  pursued once the live app's animation was in hand.
- *Hover missed moves.* Over the tiles, of 35 moves the pointer monitor heard, `DockView`'s tracking area
  heard 10. dock2 is a non-activating accessory, so the window server sends moves to the frontmost app (the
  events arrived through the global monitor, aimed at the stage's own window number); an `.activeAlways`
  tracking area gets only some, typically the first after a pause. The label stayed on the last tile until
  a later move got through — then crossfaded from it. Of ten tiles visited by glides, the settled label was
  right for five.

Hover is now driven by `PointerMonitor`, which `DockController` already ran for auto-hide. The monitor
passes each move's `mouseEventWindowUnderMousePointer`; `DockController` hands `DockView` the point only
when that is the stage, and `DockView` turns it into a slot as before, keeping its hover state, `onHover`,
and the clears on a menu or a drag. The tracking area is gone. Checked before relying on it: the field is
set on global and local events alike, reads the stage over a tile, the window below where the stage is
transparent (beside the platter, or once the dock has slid away), and the menu where a tile's menu covers
a tile. A location test alone would have shown labels under anything above the dock; the tracking area
never did, and this keeps that.

After: ten of ten settled labels right, by glides and by jumps; each switch one clean frame, 17–25 ms after
the move that reached the tile. A tile's menu hides the label, no label shows for a tile the menu covers,
and the next move after Esc labels the tile under the pointer. The suite passes.

## Observations

- Each `show` rebuilds the label's glass tree twice — setting `recipe` rebuilds, and so does the view's new
  size — measured at 0.1–0.4 ms of main thread each. Not the fault and left alone; a label whose glass
  never resized and whose recipe changed only with the appearance would move its two shapes instead.
- The system Dock's label could not be recorded as a reference: synthetic moves, with deltas or pushed into
  the edge, did not reveal it on this macOS. dock2's code already meant the label to snap.
- Hover and the ring share the one label: a hover ending hides the label of a ringed or echoed tile until
  the ring moves. Unchanged here.
- A relayout hides the label but leaves `DockView`'s hovered slot set, so the tile under a resting pointer
  stays unlabelled until it leaves. Unchanged here.
