# Dock parity: the tiles, the pointer, auto-hide

> Change `1792485673` — 2026-10-20

## Goal

Roadmap step 1: a dock at parity with the Dock's everyday behaviour — Finder, pinned and running tiles with
separators, the tile menus, dragging to reorder, pin and unpin, dropping files on tiles, its own auto-hide
with the reveal and hide measured off the system Dock, and settings copied from the system Dock.

## Implementation

**Measured first.** A ScreenCaptureKit recorder took the bottom of the screen at 60 frames a second, with
the pointer action timed inside the recorder itself (the first attempts, launching a helper process, put
40 ms of noise into every delay). Against the system Dock, at its defaults:

- *Reveal*: the pointer touching the bottom row anywhere along the screen — one event is enough — starts
  the slide 214–231 ms later (four trials). A single synthetic move to the edge moved the cursor but
  revealed nothing; moves with their deltas set do.
- *Hide*: the revealed Dock's region is the whole width below the platter's top. It slid away 57–59 ms
  after the pointer rose past that line, whether the pointer stopped 6, 20 or 40 pt above it — a delay, not
  a boundary further up.
- *Slide*: one fit for six slides both ways: 240 ms, cubic Bézier (0.35, 0.15, 0.65, 0.85), RMS 1.4 pt
  (linear 2.3, ease-in-ease-out 1.5 at 300 ms).
- *Launch hop*: a probe app that takes three seconds to finish launching hopped every 700 ms, 24 pt high at
  tile 95, in parabolas with no pause between them, the last landing as the launch finished. The old
  half-tile, 0.6 s guess was twice too high.
- *Insertion*: a new tile grows in place while the platter widens over ~360 ms on the slide's curve.
- *Separator*: 0.45 of a tile (44.55 pt at 95, matching the model), a 1 pt line from the slot's centre,
  85 pt tall, centred; it took 57 levels off the glass, a plus-darker blend. This Mac is in the light
  appearance — the white menu bar is the wallpaper's doing, which had misled the first reading.
- The system Dock on macOS 27 puts running unpinned apps straight after the pinned ones with no separator;
  the product's separator there is kept as a deliberate difference.

dock2 was then recorded the same way: reveal 206–226 ms, hide ~50 ms, slides on the same curve to RMS
0.3 pt, hops 24 pt every 700 ms. `asyncAfter` had made the reveal wander (217–256 ms); timer sources with a
millisecond's leeway fixed it.

**The model and the view.** `DockModel.compose` is the product's tile rules as a pure function; `AutoHide`
the reveal-and-hide rules as a reducer whose timers are effects, both unit-tested. `DockView` diffs the
row by identity; `DockController` owns the inputs (settings, `RunningApps`, `AppDirectory`), launches,
menus and drags.

**Three rendering traps**, each found by bisecting a recording:

- *Blending.* Probes showed a compositing filter blends only within its own group: a plus-darker layer over
  grey in the same layer gave 75 from 121; in a sibling view, or inside any plain container layer, it gave
  its own unblended 209. The fix is `allowsGroupBlending` off on the containers (private; confined to
  `Glass/`, checked with `responds(to:)`), and the dock's layers in an overlay inside the glass's own
  view. This also means the old dark-mode dot, in a sibling view of its glass, can never have blended: its
  "plus-lighter of 105" showed as a plain 124.
- *A tile added never appeared*, and the platter never widened: the new icon was given its grow-from
  scale before its `frame` was set again, so its bounds came out a hundred times too large and the row's
  update failed part-way. Layers are now moved by bounds and position.
- *Every tile cross-faded* between its old and new place on any insertion — Core Animation's implicit
  `sublayers` transition on the container, triggered by adding a layer inside an animating transaction.
  Disabled on the container; a new tile's own growth is an explicit animation, since implicit ones do not
  run on a layer added in the same commit.

**Running apps.** A launching app is in `runningApplications` (and regular) at once, but finishing its
launch changes nothing there; `isFinishedLaunching` is observed per app instead, which is what stops the
hop.

**Testing input.** AppKit's drag loop ignored every synthetic mouse-up from a tool that had touched AppKit
(`NSScreen`, `NSEvent.mouseLocation`), even when the event was otherwise identical to one it accepted from a
CoreGraphics-only tool; the tools are CoreGraphics only now. Probes that need LaunchServices to know them
(a document handler) must live outside `/private/tmp`, which it does not register.

Verified on this Mac, with probe apps standing in for the user's: auto-hide's reveal from anywhere on the
edge, hold in the band, hide above it; a probe launching (grow, hops, landing, dot) and quitting (shrink);
the menus of the platter, an app and Finder; Keep in Dock; reordering both ways and unpinning by dragging
off; an app bundle dragged from a window pinned at the gap — the drag itself revealing the hidden dock; a
text file dropped on a tile that claims it opening there, the tile highlighted while it hovered; settings
written from outside applied live. The pins were restored afterwards and the probe app unregistered and
removed; `com.apple.dock` matches the backup.

## Observations

- Dark mode is unverified for the dot and the separator, now that blending actually happens.
- The separator's height is `tile − 10`, from one tile size; `platter − 30` fits it too, and the two part
  below tile 48 and above 95.
- The hop's rise is a little steeper than the system Dock's in its first 100 ms (2–3 pt).
- `AppDirectory` keeps what it resolved for as long as dock2 runs; an app moved or registered meanwhile
  needs a restart to be seen. The app catalog (search) will watch for that.
- Not built until Files exists: Finder's tile opening Files, Show in Files, spring-loading.
