# A Dock that looks like the Dock, and gives the Dock back

> Change `1792471296` — 2026-10-19

## Goal

Prove out dock2: a replacement Dock showing Safari and Ghostty that (1) is near pixel-identical to macOS 27's
Dock, Liquid Glass included, and (2) hides the system Dock while it runs and reliably brings it back when
dock2 quits, crashes or is killed.

## Implementation

**Geometry was measured, not recalled.** The real Dock was shown at tile sizes 16, 32, 48, 64, 80, 95 and 128
and read through Accessibility (each item's frame) and screenshots (the platter's edges, the dots, the label's
outline, traced row by row at sub-pixel precision). Two findings worth keeping: the AX list frame sits 5pt
above the visible platter, whose bottom is 5pt off the screen's at every size; and the corner radius is not
proportional — the corner's outline is identical at 64, 80 and 95, and fits `min(height / 3, 28)`. The label
was taken for 13pt text until its ink was measured: "Safari" and "Ghostty" are 35.5 and 49.5pt of ink, which
only 14pt regular gives. `DockMetricsTests` pins the formulas to the AX frames.

**The glass took the longest.** `NSGlassEffectView` renders through the window server's `glassBackground`
filter, but no variant (the private `_variant`, 0–23) or style matched the Dock: the nearest, 2, lifts the
backdrop by ~+61 levels where the Dock lifts ~+30, and none drew the specular rim, because AppKit leaves the
highlight layer at opacity 0. The Dock binary links neither AppKit nor these recipes: it gets its material
from the private DesignLibrary (`DLMaterialLayerController`, a Dock-specific environment), Swift-only and out
of reach. Two dead ends on the way:

- Editing `NSGlassEffectView`'s filter by reassigning `filters` reads back correctly and changes nothing on
  screen; values only reach the window server through key paths (`filters.glassBackground.<input>`).
- A plain `CABackdropLayer` with `gaussianBlur` and `colorMatrix` matches the Dock's colour but cannot
  refract, and over high-contrast content the Dock visibly lenses its edges.

So `GlassLayer` builds `NSGlassEffectView`'s tree itself, and the recipe was fitted. A calibration window of
24pt colour stripes and hairlines was put behind the real Dock and photographed; the same window behind a
prototype was scored per stripe (colour), over edge bands (refraction, rims), and over the bare wallpaper,
and coordinate descent ran on the filter and rim parameters. The tone response it had to match is clean:
`out = 0.737 · in + 40.3` in sRGB with a saturation boost, measured from the grey stripes. Result: stripe
colours within 1.5 levels RMS, wallpaper edges within 4.6, and dock2's platter equal to the prototype's
within a level. A rim fit went nowhere until it turned out the highlight effect's angles are in the layer's
flipped space (0 lights the bottom, π/2 the right): the defaults AppKit uses had been lighting the sides.
The label needed no fit: AppKit's regular recipe reproduces the Dock's label fill to a level
(142, 141, 176 against 142, 141, 175).

**Suppression** is auto-hide with a 1000s reveal delay plus a Dock restart — the Dock has no off switch and
reads these keys only at launch. Safety comes from three pieces with one rule (the journal is the only record
of the user's settings): the journal written before any key is touched; an instance lock so exactly one
process owns it; and a guard process waiting on a kqueue for dock2's exit. Rejected: a LaunchAgent to restore
at login after power loss — writing one on every launch raises macOS's "background item added" notification
each time; the next launch and `dock2 --restore` cover that case instead. Writing a key clears it first:
restoring `tilesize` showed cfprefsd keeps an integer 95 over a real 95.0, because `CFEqual` calls them equal.

Verified on this machine: suppression and restore on SIGTERM; restore by the guard after `kill -9`; with
dock2 and the guard both killed, restore by `--restore` and by the next launch (which kept the journal's
values rather than snapshotting dock2's); single instance; `--restore` refusing while dock2 runs; launching
an app by click with its hop and running dot (Calculator, via `DOCK2_ITEMS`); idle CPU 0.0%. After all of it
`com.apple.dock` matches the backup taken before work started, types included.

## Observations

- **Dark mode is unmeasured.** The platter's dark white/black are AppKit's light-to-dark delta applied to the
  fit; the dot is white by convention. Measuring needs the system appearance switched.
- **The launch hop is a guess** (half a tile, 0.6s per hop); the real one was not recorded.
- The label's top rim pixel runs ~30 levels brighter than the Dock's; the rest of the label matches within a
  pixel.
- Below tile size 48 the platter's width is approximate: the end padding measured 10 and 4pt at 16 and 32
  against 12pt above, with no explanation found.
- dock2 is always visible and does not mirror the user's auto-hide (this machine has it on), so it covers the
  bottom of full-height windows. Auto-hide is the obvious next feature.
- The calibration tooling (pattern window, prototype server, optimiser) was built in a scratch directory and
  is not in the tree; re-fitting after a macOS update would want it committed.
