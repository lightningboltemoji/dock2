# Debug pointer and chords

> Change `1792565746` — 2026-10-20

## Goal

`dock2 debug` could type and press plain keys, but not much else. Chords (⌘K, ⌘1–9, ⌘W), ⌘ held for the
number badges, and the pointer were out of reach: hover and labels, clicking a tile or a row, right-click
menus, drag and drop, and the pointer revealing the dock. The user wanted those next, so the dock itself could
be tested the way the panel now is.

## Implementation

**Keys and chords stay inside dock2.** `debug key` takes a key or a chord (`ControlPress`: `cmd+k`,
`shift+cmd+.`, `cmd++`, `option+up`) and `debug hold` takes modifiers alone. The router builds the events with
US-layout key codes, since Files' columns and ⌘↑/⌘↓ match on codes, and posts them into dock2's own queue with
`NSApp.postEvent`. The plain keys were first sent straight to the stage window. A posted event instead goes
through `NSApplication.sendEvent`, so a chord reaches the key-equivalent path. An open context menu's
tracking loop takes it too: Esc closes a menu the pointer opened. `type` moved to the same path. A chord
posted to the HID stream was rejected, because with dock2 not holding the keyboard ⌘W or ⌘K goes to the
terminal.

**The pointer is the real one.** Feeding synthetic mouse events into dock2's own queue was considered and
rejected. `PointerMonitor` and the dock's hover and auto-hide read `NSEvent.mouseLocation`, so a faked
pointer would leave dock2 split between two positions. Tile drags are `NSDraggingSession`s, which follow the
hardware. So `move`, `click` and `drag` post to the HID stream from the CLI, aimed at a `PointerTarget`
resolved in `debug state`: a tile by name or place, a row from the field up, the field, the platter, the
screen's bottom edge or a point. A target `state` says is not showing is refused, so a click aimed at a hidden
dock does not land in whatever is beneath. These commands move the person's cursor, and say so.

**State grew** the tiles (name as its label says it, kind, running, ringed, frame) and each row's frame. A
row scrolled out of the panel has none.

**Verified.** `ControlTests` cover chord parsing, holds, and targets resolving to where `state` says, or
nowhere when not showing. Live, with `DOCK2_DEBUG=1` and the user's hands off:

- `debug move edge` revealed the dock through auto-hide, and dock2 took the keyboard.
- `debug move tile:Fiber` showed Fiber's label.
- `debug click tile:Files --right` opened Files' menu (Home … Open in Finder), and `debug key escape` closed
  it.
- With dock2 summoned, `debug hold cmd` showed the badges 1–3, and `none` took them away.
- `debug type saf` then `debug key cmd+k` opened the top hit's actions (Open, Show in Files, Keep in Dock).
- `debug click row:7` selected "Show all … files" and `--double` opened Files on the search.
- `debug drag tile:3` to 40 pt right of it, recorded at 120 fps: the press darkened the icon, a drag session
  carried a copy under the pointer, and the order was unchanged after the drop.

## Observations

- Two runs were spoiled by the user moving the mouse mid-run, which dismisses dock2 or moves the pointer
  off target. A person's input is not yet told apart from these commands' events; emira's director tags its
  own and stops at anything untagged.
- The first row click looked like a failure, but a single click selects and a double-click opens. Clicking a
  result that opens an app or a file was not tried, to keep the runs free of side effects.
- A drag off the dock unpins a tile, so the drag tried stayed on the dock. Dropping files onto a tile, and
  springing Files open by holding over Finder's tile, were not run.
- File results arrive after the first frame. A script waiting for a row polls `debug state`. A
  `debug wait` would make that a command.
