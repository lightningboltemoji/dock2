# The sideline

> Change `1793429427` — 2026-10-30

## Goal

Some apps have to run without being wanted, Outlook and Zoom among them, and their tiles spent the day as
full-size icons beside the apps that were chosen. `PLAN-SIDELINE.md` designed the answer: a sidelined app's tile
moves onto a small glass puck at the dock's right end, still a tile in every way, so the dock keeps only the
apps that were chosen. With it came three things the puck needs: tile sizes up to 256, a floor under badges,
and a dock that fits the screen, which dock2 never did.

## Implementation

**The two measurements came first**, on the real Dock, dock2 quit and `com.apple.dock` exported before and
imported after (the domain diffed identical), with a test app badged by a distributed notification.

- *Badges at small sizes.* At tile sizes 16, 24, 32, 48 and 64 the Dock's badge was 12, 18, 26, 38 and 50 px
  across at 2×: 6, 9, 13, 19 and 25pt, 25/64 of the tile at every size. Apple sets no floor, so the 16pt
  floor is dock2's own, and it applies to the row's badges below tile 41 as well as the puck's.
- *How close the Dock's ends come to the screen's edges.* On the 1800 × 1169pt screen at tile size 128, test
  apps launched one at a time: eleven tiles fit at full size, and from twelve the Dock shrank. The margin
  is no constant. At every count from 12 tiles to 39, Finder's slot sat a slot less 6pt from the screen's
  edge (slot width = 1812 / (N + 2.45), N tiles and a separator, to 0.01pt). Asking for 112 or 96 instead of
  128 changed nothing once it shrank. In dock2's terms the platter's end stays `slot − 6 − (inset + 6)` in,
  119pt at 128 (`DockMetrics.screenMargin`). `FittingTests` pins all fifteen readings.

**The packing** is `SidelinePacking`, pure, in Core. A cell is the slot over a whole number; its icon is the
cell less the icon's own inset, solved over insets of 1, 2 and 3 (`icon(inSlot:)`). The finest cut is the
largest whose icon is 24pt or more. The cuts in play are the coarsest whose square holds every app (halves
at least) and the next finer, both capped at the finest. Past the finest, that cut alone, as wide as it has to be. Every
shape within the limits is ranked by empty cells, then cut, then rows. Brute force, since the limits keep it to a few
dozen shapes. The two-cut limit is the design's: left free, the fewest-empty-cells rule would always shrink to
fill a rectangle (five apps as a column of fifths, 25pt beside 128pt), so one empty cell is worth one size
step. Below tile 48 there is no cut: one row of 24pt icons, or the tiles' size.

**The layout** keeps the row one-dimensional and adds the puck after it: `DockLayout.slots` holds the row's,
then the puck's cells in fill order (`rowCount`, `isSidelined`), and hit-testing is by frame
(`contains`, `slotIndex`), which for the row's full-height slots is the old x test. The puck's height is its
rows and the platter's rims (`platterRim`, 6 or 8); its width its columns and the platter's margins, rounded up
to whole points, the cells centred in it; the gap `puckGap`, 12 magnified and rounded.

Its corners are the platter's, unscaled. The design scaled them to the puck's height (15.5 for one app at 128),
and next to the platter's 28 that read as another object. The platter's radius has two consequences, both in
`puckSize`. The puck is never smaller than its corners, at least `2 × cornerRadius` each way, so one app at 64
sits in a 56pt round puck. And a full-height puck's top right badge sits deep in the corner's curve: at 128 in
fifths, 0.8pt from the outline. Measured with a script over every size and cut: the top edge clears every
badge by 5.7pt or more from tile 48, so only the corner fails, and only width is free, since the height is
capped by the platter's. So the puck widens just enough for that badge's circle to clear the curve by
`puckBadgeClearance`, solved in closed form from the badge's depth below the top. It also grows upward where
it has the height, which only the one-row pucks below tile 48 need. Rejected: shrinking the cells for more
rim, which at 128 fifths pushes the icons under 24pt before it clears the corner; shrinking the icons inside
their cells, which moves a floored badge in by only 0.3 of the shrink.

The clearance is 4pt. 2 and 3 were computed too: the 5×2 puck at 128 is 76, 80 and 83pt wide for 2, 3 and 4,
against 72 with scaled corners, and at 4 it reads clear on the glass where 0.8 read as touching. Below tile 25
nothing reaches 4: the puck is the platter's height, the icons the tiles' size, and the platter's own row
badges are 2.1pt from its top at tile 16 and 3.7 at 24. There the puck's badges are as close as the row's. `top(of:)` gives the label and the menu the puck's top over its tiles.

**Fitting** is in `DockLayout.fit`, so every layout fits its screen, the onboarding preview's included. It
tries the dock centred with the rule's puck. If that fails, it tries finer cuts, each as narrow as that cut
goes with the fewest empty cells at that width. Then the finest cut, the platter slid left just enough, on
whole points. Then a bisection over tile size, to 0.01pt, for the largest size at which those fit. For the row
alone that is monotone, since the inset's steps only widen it. With a puck it is not quite, since the finest
cut grows with the size, so the search lands on a size that fits rather than certainly the largest. The
layout's `metrics` are the fitted ones, and the controller's hop heights and number badges read them.

**Past 128** every length is its value at 128 times `size / 128` (`magnification`, the formulas taken at
`min(size, 128)`). The Dock's own formulas stop holding there: its corners stay 28. Snow Leopard's numbers
were already in proportion. The window picture's capture size follows `tileSizes.upperBound`, so it
is 480 px now.

**The model.** `compose(sidelined:)` drops sidelined apps from the row; `sideline` lists the running ones in
the list's order. A file listing an app in both keys puts it on the puck: the menu can't write that, so only a
hand edit does. `DockChoices` makes every move exclusive, and `SettingsStore.dockChoices` writes both keys. An
equal write is a no-op and observers hear the change after the turn, so a move relays out once, on the final
lists. Search's Keep in Dock goes through it too.

**The glass.** The puck is a third shape of the stage's glass, set with the platter's in one `setGlass`. The
shape never goes away: `GlassLayer` builds its tree again whenever the number of shapes changes, ending
whatever was playing, and a tree built mid-change cannot animate a bud. With nothing on it, the shape is a 2pt
speck half the platter's height in from its right end. That is at least 14pt inside the outline at tile 16,
past the merge's smoothness of 8, so the smooth union leaves the platter's outline alone. Rejected: tucking
it in as a copy of the platter's end, where two equal distances under the smooth minimum push the outline out
by smoothness / 4, 2pt. The bud is then the ordinary reshape from the speck. Reduce Transparency's fill and
Increase Contrast's border are the union's path, where the speck vanishes too.

**The tiles** need no new drawing: a puck tile is a `TileLayer` at its icon's size. The badge (floored,
centred on the Dock's circle, capped at the wider of the icon and itself), the hop and the inset follow from
`DockMetrics(tileSize: icon)`. Its slot's dot is empty, so no dot shows while `running` stays true for VoiceOver
and debug state. A tile keeps its layer between the row and the puck, so it flies across, re-rendered at its new
size. Ring walks run the row then the puck. ⌘1–9 and the number badges take the row alone, and the echo takes
both. Puck tiles cannot be picked up, and a tile dragged over the puck lands at the end of the pinned apps.
VoiceOver finds a group, "Sideline", after the list's items. The menu's Sideline is a check and shows only on
the glass: the shelf ignores sidelining.

**Validation.** `SidelineTests` takes the design's tables as cases and checks every size from 16 to 256 against
1 to 30 apps: never taller than the platter, never under 24pt, never wider than a tile until the finest cut is
full, empty cells only at the top right, only the cuts in play. It also covers the puck's geometry and hit-testing,
magnification, the badge floor, fitting against the measured Dock, and the model. A contact sheet from
the real files at 48 to 256 against 1 to 12 apps matched the tables. On the glass, on a build with its own
config and test apps: the gap, no merge at rest, the bud in and out (it merges for a frame or two and pulls
apart), the platter's corners on the puck at 128, 64 and 48 (round at the last two), the 5×2 puck's top right
badge 4pt clear of the curve, the label over a puck tile, badges floored on 25pt
icons, the dark appearance, Reduce Transparency with Increase Contrast. The menu worked both ways: sidelining a
pinned app unpinned it, and unchecking returned it to the running apps in launch order. Also seen: the ring
walking onto the puck, the echo, numbers on the row only, the accessibility tree, the puck kept with running
apps hidden, and the slide away. At 192 the rule's puck fit centred. At 256 every step showed: sixths to stay
centred; then, with twelve sidelined, tenths and the dock slid 6pt to land the puck exactly on the 238pt
margin; then, with a fifth tile on the row, slots of 244.4pt with both ends at that size's margin.

## Observations

- At 256 the puck gives first, by the design's order, so three apps beside a four-tile row are 43pt icons
  beside 256pt ones: the contrast the two-cut limit exists to avoid. Sliding before narrowing, or narrowing only
  so far, may read better; unjudged.
- The label over a puck tile stands on the puck's top, lower than the row's labels when the puck is short. The
  design did not say; it read well in the one capture.
- The shrink rule was measured on one screen width. A launching sidelined app's hop was not seen (the test apps
  finish launching at once), nor the puck under Reduce Motion, nor the onboarding preview fitted to its bounds.
- At 256, Files' panel over a 300pt platter tops out 874pt above the screen's bottom on this 1169pt screen:
  worked out from `PanelMetrics`, not seen.
- The fitted tile size is the layout's alone; the setting keeps what was asked.
- Nothing in the Settings window: the menu and the file, as designed for now.
