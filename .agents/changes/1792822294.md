# Filling the Finder tile's picture

> Change `1792822294` — 2026-10-23

## Goal

The Finder tile's Cairo picture was white ink alone. Cairo drew its pictures black on white paper, so a
strawberry has a body and a TV has a screen. The Fill toggle draws the picture that way: its ink black and
what it encloses white. It is on by default.

## Implementation

**What is inside is computed.** `PixelArt.inside` flood-fills the paper from the picture's edge and returns
what the fill never reached. Paper reaches paper only through a side. Cairo's lines step diagonally all the
time (the strawberry's whole outline does), and a flood that also crossed corners would leak through every
step and fill almost nothing. All 59 pictures were rendered both ways on a contact sheet. None needed a mask
drawn by hand, so none has one: the rule is the whole design, and a picture added later gets filled for free.

**What it gives.** Most pictures come out as Cairo's own, black on white: the fruit, the egg, the bottle,
the faces, the house, the TV, the bulb, the bandage, the baseball. Holes count as inside, so the anchor's
ring and the fish's eye are white, as Cairo's paper was. What encloses nothing turns into a black
silhouette: the cross, both fish, the frog, the crab, the waves, the resistor, the revolver, the dinosaur,
the cup and saucer, the note, the fir tree and the shamrock. That was accepted when the fill was asked for
("some can't be filled"). The rule stays the same for every picture rather than keeping exceptions white.

**One layer.** The black and the white are drawn inside the one transparency layer that already carried the
shadow. The shadow falls from the whole shape, and the white casts none onto the black.

**Stored in `FinderIcon`.** `filled` is part of what the tile wears, beside the picture and the colour, and
the picker's cells are drawn with it. A synthesized decoder needs every key and would refuse an icon stored
before `filled` existed, which drops the user's picture back to Finder's. The custom decoder reads a missing
key as filled, the default. The toggle, "Fill", sits under the colour well and is disabled for Finder's own
icon, as the well is.

**Checked.** An offline harness built from the same sources drew every picture both ways at 128 px. Close
up at 200 px it checked the grapes, the rose, the cactus, the trees, the crab, the frog, the woman and the
treble clef. It also checked 32 and 64 px. In the debug dock at 128 pt in the Snow Leopard style, the
user's strawberry loaded from a setting stored without the key. A `defaults write … -dict-add filled` swapped
the tile with no restart, reflection included. The toggle in the real Settings window, pressed through
Accessibility, wrote `filled = 0` and the dock followed. These runs used the label "Fill the picture" and
off as the default, the first cut. The rename and the default were tested by unit tests only.

## Observations

- The black is pure black, not fitted to anything Icon Composer does with a dark layer. On a dark colour
  it barely stands off the plate, and the white carries the shape. That is unmeasured.
- At 16 pt, where a picture scales down, the black and white blur to grey. The white picture blurs too.
- What a filled picture becomes in the system's Dark, Clear and Tinted styles is open (`design/files.md`).
