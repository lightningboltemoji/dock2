# ⌃U ⌃D read a view

> Change `1794465334` — 2026-11-11

## Goal

Vim's half-page scroll keys, ⌃U and ⌃D. A list with a preview has two things that scroll, the rows and the preview,
so the question was which one the keys move, and whether dock2 needs focus between panes to decide.

## Implementation

The keys belong to a role, not to a pane. A screen has at most one selection and at most one thing to read, so a key
for each role always has exactly one target, and nothing has to hold focus or show that it does. ↑ ↓ (and ⌃N ⌃P,
which the text system already turns into `moveDown:`/`moveUp:`) move the selection. ⌃U ⌃D move the reading: a detail
on its own, or a list's preview. This is telescope.nvim's split: its prompt keeps focus while ⌃U/⌃D scroll the
preview. Mail does the same with space, paging the message while the list has focus.

On a list without a preview the keys do nothing to the rows; they stay the field's. Falling back to moving the selection
was considered and rejected: a list you were paging through with ⌃D would then stop answering the moment a pushed
view put a preview beside it. Tab between panes was rejected too. Tab already means the next field in a form and
completion in the path bar. A focused pane needs an indicator, and the ring is for tiles. And typing while the preview
held focus would either take focus back or do nothing.

⌃U and ⌃D are matched as chords (`Chord.readUp`, `readDown`), not as text-system selectors. ⌃U is bound to nothing in
`StandardKeyBinding.dict`, so it never reaches `doCommandBy`. ⌃D arrives as `deleteForward:`, which fn⌫ sends too. They
arrive through `performKeyEquivalent` into `Component.keyEquivalent`. That covers the field-led list and the standalone
detail, which holds first responder itself. `DetailComponent.read` moves the clip view half its height, constrained by
`constrainBoundsRect`, and flashes the overlay scroller. It's instant, like every other keyboard scroll in dock2, which
also keeps up with key repeat. It claims the chord even when there's nothing to scroll, so whether ⌃D deletes in the
field never depends on how long the item's text is. Of vim's scroll keys only this pair comes cheap: ⌃F ⌃B ⌃E ⌃Y are
caret moves and yank in the field, and ⌃D's forward delete does nothing with the caret at the end of a query.

A list's preview now opens each new item at its top (`ListComponent.previewed`). Before, the clip view kept its offset
across items, and a long item reached straight from a scrolled long item opened partway down. A redraw of the same item
keeps its place.

## Observations

- Tested in the golden-gate VM on the demo plugin's Previews and Text Alone. ⌃D and ⌃U move half the visible height and
  stop at the ends. Beside a preview ⌃D leaves the field's text alone; on a list without one it deletes forward. Tested
  through `debug key` and through real-shaped ⌃-chords posted to the HID tap.
- Seen with the reset taken out and put back: clicking from a scrolled Text That Scrolls to Everything opened Everything
  scrolled; with the reset it opens at the top.
- A ⌃-chord posted with `postToPid` and its control character set reached neither the preview nor the field. Why wasn't
  established; `OBSERVING.md` now points at the HID tap.
- Paging the list itself isn't built. fn↑ fn↓ (`scrollPageUp:`/`scrollPageDown:`), ⌥fn↑ ⌥fn↓ and ⌃V (`pageUp:`/
  `pageDown:`) already reach `Component.command` as selectors and nothing handles them. They're the natural keys for it,
  and they leave ⌃U ⌃D with the reading.
- Files and search are untouched: Files has Finder's keys, and search has nothing to read.
