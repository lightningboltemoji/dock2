# A click elsewhere reaches what it was aimed at

> Change `1792551569` — 2026-10-20

## Goal

Summoned with ⌘space under auto-hide, dock2 took every click on the screen: one aimed at a window behind it
went to the stage, and dock2 stayed. The design says a click outside dock2 dismisses it, and the click
should land where it was aimed.

## Implementation

Auto-hide's slide set `stage.window.ignoresMouseEvents = !shown`. A probe settled what that does: a
dock-level, transparent `NSPanel` over a window of the probe's own, with a synthetic click on a transparent
pixel. Left alone, the click goes to the window beneath. Set to `false`, even after `true`, the panel takes
it. Once set, the property replaces hit-testing by alpha, and no public API restores it. So every reveal left
the full-screen stage catching every click. A dock revealed by the pointer hides as soon as the pointer leaves,
so only engagement, which holds the dock up, exposed the problem. The probe found that key status,
`.fullScreenAuxiliary`, a focused field, the field's shadow and the glass itself all pass clicks. Nothing sets
the property now, which `StageWindow` records.

The flag existed for the hidden dock. Without it, a click on the screen's bottom row under the platter went
to dock2. A capture of the stage window found one device-pixel row of the platter's outer rim (outset 0.5)
left at the edge, but sliding further by that outset did not help. Probing the real `GlassLayer` showed why:
the window server routes clicks to glass up to 1.5 pt beyond its shape, with or without rims, and passes them
at 2 pt. That reaches past anything the glass draws. Glass at alpha 0, or hidden, passes clicks even inside
its shape. So once the platter has slid away, the glass goes to alpha 0, along with the overlay and the tiles
on it. That is the state Reduce Motion's hidden dock was already in. The glass comes back before the slide
in, and while the alpha changes the platter is below the screen. The completion of a slide that a later one
superseded does nothing, counted as the panel's fades are.

Rejected: padding the slide by a margin, since the 1.5–2 pt is a property of private glass internals and the
travel is measured; and ordering the stage out while hidden, which would mean window-server churn on every
reveal.

Verified on this Mac with a probe window behind dock2 and a listen-only event tap that names each click's
target process. Summoned: clicks mid-screen, at a corner, beside the field and in its shadow reached the
probe and dismissed dock2, and a click on the platter stayed dock2's. Hidden: clicks on the bottom row under
the platter, and 2, 6 and 20 pt up, reached the probe. The same held under `DOCK2_REDUCE_MOTION=1`, where
the faded platter in place passed clicks. With the slide slowed fivefold, the glass was drawn throughout the
slide out, gone after it and back on reveal. A hide interrupted by a reveal settled drawn, and a reveal
interrupted by a hide settled clear. Against the same probe, the previous build sent a summoned click
mid-screen to dock2 and kept the keyboard.

## Observations

- A relayout that reshapes the platter during the slide out replaces the slide's animation, and the
  transaction's completion runs then, so the dock would clear just short of the edge. Not seen.
- Glass catches clicks a point or two past its edge when shown too, beside the platter and the field. It
  is too small to matter, and it is the window server's doing.
- With `slideTimeModifier` at 0 the hide has no animation, and the glass clears at once. Reasoned, not seen.
