# The tile numbers: in the dot's place, whenever ⌘ is held

> Change `1793854001` — 2026-11-04

## Goal

The number badges were loud and came and went for no reason anyone could see. A glass capsule stood on the top edge of
each icon, and ⌘space with ⌘ kept down never showed them at all.

## Implementation

**When.** They hung on edges dock2 could miss, each its own way of showing nothing. A ⌘ pressed before dock2 had the
keyboard sends it no `flagsChanged`, so a held ⌘space, or a pointer reveal with ⌘ down, armed nothing. Any key cancelled
them with nothing to re-arm while ⌘ stayed down, so ⌘A and then holding showed nothing either. And a chord dock2 handles
goes through `performKeyEquivalent` and never reaches `sendEvent`, so ⌘K left them up. Now they are level-triggered:
`InteractionController.wantsNumbers` asks `NSEvent.modifierFlags` what is down, and every event only says to look again.

The wait is read again as it ends. ⌘ let go while the keyboard is changing hands goes to the app that had it, and without
that second look the numbers would come up and stay until the next key. It runs 0.4 s from the last key rather than 0.3:
for ⌘space that key is the space, and a quick ⌘space should be over before they show. How long a hand stays on ⌘ was
not measured, so 0.4 is a judgement, in one constant, `numberDelay`.

They stay limited to dock2 having the keyboard. ⌘1–9 reach a tile only then, and with the dock on screen, any ⌘ held for
any reason in any app would number it. A menu is out by `NSMenu`'s tracking notifications rather than `MenuBuilder.onOpen`,
which only the dock's menus set: the search's, Files' and the path bar's matter as much, since a menu takes ⌘ let go too.

Rejected: a pure reducer beside `Interaction`. The part that fixes this is reading the modifiers at the moment dock2 takes
the keyboard, which a reducer would only be told about; what is left is one condition and one timer. A global
`flagsChanged` monitor was not tried: it would hear ⌘ early, but the numbers need nothing from before the keyboard is
dock2's, and reading the current state takes no permission.

**Where.** `BadgesView`, a full-stage overlay above the dock, is gone. A number is a `CATextLayer` per tile in `DockView`'s
`content`, so it has the dot's blend (plus-lighter in dark), slides with the dock, and moves with its tile through a relayout
without the controller redrawing anything. `placeMark` is the dot's old code in `place` with the number beside it, so a
tile has one answer to what stands under it, and a digit replaces the dot on a running tile rather than sitting by it.

The type steps with the tile, 8, 9, 10 and 11 pt, where the first try was 10 throughout: at tile size 16 that ran into the
icon, with 7 pt under it. On the shelf the number takes the glass's sizes rather than `shelfScale`, which would make it
23 pt on a 128 tile.

`dock2 debug hold` used to post a `flagsChanged` and nothing else, which a read of the hardware's state would not see. It
now also sets `debugHeld`, so held before `summon` it stands for ⌘space kept down, and `debug state` gives each tile's
`number`.

Measured through `debug hold`, polling `debug state`: ⌘ down before the summon shows them at 0.41 s, after a fresh launch
and after a minute idle; let go at 0.2 s, never; a fresh ⌘, 0.42 s; a key, gone and back at 0.41 s; ⌘⇧ gone, and back at
0.41 s from letting ⇧ go; no keyboard, none; dismissed while showing or while waiting, gone with nothing left armed; a menu
up, gone, still gone 0.15 s after it closes, and back by 0.7 s. Looked at: light at 16, 48 and 128, dark at 48 and 128, and
Snow Leopard at 56 in light and 128 in dark.

## Observations

- The real keyboard was never in the loop on ⌘: it needs a ⌘ posted at the HID tap, and the Mac was in use. `debug hold`
  stands in for `NSEvent.modifierFlags`, so a real ⌘space held down has not been seen working. `keyActivity` hearing real
  keys was, by accident: a person's typing during the runs read as the numbers blinking off, until a log showed plain `.`
  and delete ([observing](../OBSERVING.md#running-the-build-under-observation)).
- The setting is still `dock.number-badges`, and `Settings.numberBadges`: renaming it would need a migration of the file.
- In the light appearance over something dark the digits are dark on dark, as the dots are. The numbers carry more than a
  dot does, so this is the first thing to look at if they prove too faint. Its contrast was not measured over a bright
  backdrop or under Increase Contrast.
- At tile size 16 a digit stands against the icon: there is no smaller size that reads.
- The 0.15 s fade is the same under Reduce Motion, which was not tried.
- Read from the code and not tried: ⌘⇥ with dock2 up shows the numbers under the switcher until the new app comes forward.
