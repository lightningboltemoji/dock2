# Snow Leopard, a second style for the dock

> Change `1792814708` — 2026-10-23

## Goal

A throwback: the dock as Mac OS X 10.6 drew it, chosen in Settings → Dock beside Liquid Glass. The icons
stand on a shelf in perspective that mirrors them and the screen above it, a blue light glows under each
running app, and a dashed lane runs across the shelf for a separator. Like the glass, it is measured rather
than remembered. With no Snow Leopard to run, the source is one screenshot of its Dock.

## Implementation

**The source.** One screenshot of 10.6.8 on a PowerBook G4 15": 1280 × 854 at 1×, so a pixel is a point, at
tile size 56. It carries the "Color LCD" profile and was converted to sRGB with ColorSync (Pillow's
`ImageCms`) before any tone was read. The conversion lifts the greys by about 19 levels, and its values are
what the screenshot looks like on this Mac. Pixel profiles were read with Pillow, NumPy and SciPy.

**Geometry.** Everything is in `DockMetrics` behind its new `style`, and `DockLayout` adds the shelf.
`DockLayout.platter` stays what is hit-tested, slid away and stood on by the label. On the shelf it is the
back edge's width, from the screen's edge up to the icons' squares' tops. The table gives the values at tile
size 56.
Every one grows in proportion to the tile size, since one screenshot cannot say how they scale.

| | At tile 56 | Measured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| tile | 60 apart, `tile + 2 × inset` | tile centres; the light centres at 86, 387, 927, 987 |
| back edge | 47.4 above the screen's edge; the run plus 4.5 either side | row 806 is 0.38 covered; x 50–1230 around a run 1171 wide |
| ends | leaning out 0.59 for each point down | 27 px over the 46 rows to the screen's edge |
| lip | the last 3: a 1 pt seam over 2 pt of light | rows 851, 852–853 |
| icons | their art on a line 20.5 up | the icons' reflection axis, row 833.5 (below) |
| art in its square | 824 of 1024, centred, so 5.5 in from each side | ten apps' icons drawn at 1024 px, their alpha bounds |
| mirror line | 48 up, 0.6 above the back edge | stars and the window reflected at row `1611 − y` (below) |
| separator slot | 31 | 987 → 1078 across it, 91 for 60 |
| footprint | 71.0 | the icon line, less the art's margin, plus the tile |

Snow Leopard's icons filled their squares and stood on the line: in the screenshot their art is 48–54 tall
and ends at rows 830–834. A macOS 27 icon's art sits 5.5 inside its square, so with the square on the line
the icons hovered above their own reflections. On the shelf the square drops by that margin and the art
stands on the line. `ShelfTests` pins each of these to the screenshot. The margins were really 6 on the left and 2–3 on the right:
the run sat about 1.5 right of the shelf's centre. That is unexplained, and the shelf is drawn centred.

**The floor.** Nothing behind it shows through. In the gaps between icons, the additive model `T + ρ·M`, where
M is the wallpaper at the mirrored row, was fitted by row across columns and all three channels at once. The
light tone runs 118 → 174 from the back edge to the lip; the dark one runs 97.5 → 129.5.

They meet along an S, the "s-curve" of Leopard's floor pictures, `scurve-sm`, `-m`, `-l` and `-xl`
([MacMost](https://macmost.com/customizing-your-dock.html),
[Creative Techs](https://www.creativetechs.com/2008/04/14/customizing-your-leopard-dock/)). Copies of all four
are on GitHub ([lachyman22.github.io](https://github.com/lachyman22/lachyman22.github.io/tree/main/assets/dockbg)),
at the sizes MacMost gives and tagged Generic RGB like Leopard's own resources. They were measured, not
shipped:

- They are one picture at four sizes. Each is a rectangle with the whole S, from the back edge to the screen's
  edge, and their tones, alpha and S agree within 0.007 of the height.
- Stretched end to end across the shelf at every height, a picture puts the step within 0.24 px rms of the
  screenshot's, in twenty gap columns. Laid a pixel to a point and centred, it misses by 1.1. Fitted freely,
  its scale comes out as the shelf's width halfway down.
- So on a short dock the whole S squeezes into a few tiles, as Leopard's did.

`DockLayout.Shelf.floorStep` is a degree-7 fit to the four, within 0.28 pt at tile 56. The first build fitted
the screenshot alone, along the back edge and held flat past its ends. On a short, deep shelf that flattened
the S under both leaning ends, which the user spotted at tile 128.

The seam is 96. The lip's light edge is an arch, read along rows 852–853 away from the lights: `235 − 27u²`,
with `u` 1 at the back edge's ends. `Shelf` draws all of it as one picture (`floorPicture`) cut to the
trapezoid. It is drawn at the size the shelf is moving to and stretched on the way there; the trapezoid's path
animates exactly.

**Reflections.** The shelf mirrors the screen about a line just above its back edge, and each icon about its
foot, both as plain flips. In the screenshot, stars above the shelf have dimmer twins at row `1611 − y`: a
mirror about the boundary between rows 805 and 806, while row 806 is the back edge's, 0.38 covered. Under the System
Profiler window, the front rows carry that window's bottom rows, mirrored, in neutral grey over a purple
wallpaper. They change exactly at the window's right edge, so the floor hides what is behind it and its tint
is reflection ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/187858/shelvedockshelf.html),
[Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2007-08-30-leopards-dock-doesnt-work-on-the-side.html)). Reflectance,
by depth from the back edge to the lip, comes from four readings:

- the stars' contrast;
- the fitted ρ;
- the slope of the icons' reflections, 0.22 at 0.76 of the depth;
- the step in the front rows at the window's right edge, x 583/584.

It is 0.32 at the back edge, 0.24 at 0.6, and 0.19 from 0.8 to the lip (rows 849–850 at the step: 0.19
and 0.20). Of the lip, the seam reflects 0.14 and the light edge 0.03. The icons' reflections show their
lowest 17.5 pt, down to the lip. Both mirrors add, with `plusL`, under a mask of that ramp, so the floor's tones are exactly what
was measured over a dark wallpaper.

- **The screen** (`ScreenMirror`, in `Glass/`): a `CABackdropLayer` from the screen's edge up to the mirror
  line's reflection of it, filtered by `displacementMap` with a ramp map. Each row of the floor samples the
  row as far above the line.
  - *Rejected, from a probe:* a transform, a `backdropRect` or a `CAPortalLayer` on a backdrop. A backdrop
    only samples what lies under where it is drawn.
  - *The map's terms, from probes over a grid:* its first row is its top, and it stretches to the layer.
    Green over `inputAmount` is the shift up, red the shift sideways, and blue 1 shows the shifted image
    alone.
  - *Three things the first builds got wrong, each found by capturing the dock over a known backdrop:* white
    lines at set heights, white posts, then the original's own scene (below).
    - Inside the masked floor, the reflection came out compressed about 2.2×. A masked layer is drawn apart
      at its own size, half the backdrop's, so the mirror is now a sibling with its own mask.
    - Tagged DeviceRGB, the map was converted to this Display P3 screen's space. Green leaked into red, and
      the posts' reflections leaned 0.48 pt sideways for each point down. A probe over a grid shifted 20 pt
      down and 8.5 sideways in sRGB or DeviceRGB, and exactly 20 and 0 in the screen's own space. The map is
      drawn in the window's colour space and drawn again when that changes.
    - At 8 bits and a row a point, the shift came in steps of about 0.4 pt. The map is now 16 bits at four
      rows a point.
  - *Measured on the live shelf afterwards:* lines every 4 pt from 22 to 42 above the back edge reflect within
    0.02 pt of where the line puts them, two of six within 0.15. White posts stay within a point at every
    depth.
- **The icons** (`LayerMirror`, in `Glass/`): a `CAPortalLayer` onto the tiles' container, flipped about the
  icon line. A probe showed it mirrors a live, animating tree: a hop moved its reflection down. It draws only
  if it comes after its source in the tree, so it goes above the tiles, masked to the floor below the icon
  line. Once on screen, a portal stops drawing when its frame or transform changes, even to the same value
  inside an animating transaction. A relayout did that, and the reflections vanished at the first tile added.
  So `LayerMirror` leaves the portal alone unless its place changes, and then puts a new one in its place.
  It was seen surviving tile sizes 56, 72 and 48 and pins and unpins. A `CAReplicatorLayer` was ruled out without being tried: its copy draws inside the replicator, under
  the same mask as the icons, so the floor beneath the reflections would come out darker by `1 − ρ`.

**The separator.** The dashes' centroids sit at rows 809.83, 812.40, 815.46, 819.16, 823.58, 828.94, 835.64
and 844.62, equally spaced in `1/(y − 776.55)` with residuals under 0.1 px. Each dash is 0.49 of its step
long and `0.29 × (y − 776.55)` wide. The lane is centred at one x on every row: it does not lean in the
shelf's perspective, so it is a picture of its own. `DockMetrics.laneDashes` builds it. Drawn with Core
Graphics and composited at 1× over the sRGB screenshot, with the original lane interpolated away, it
differs from the original by 1.73 of 255 on average at white 0.33, against 11 with no lane. White 0.32
scored 1.73 and 0.30 scored 2.09. A shift of half a point either way scored 4.0 to 4.7.

**The light.** The cleanest is under the tile at 927, over a neutral floor. It was fitted as light added to the
floor (rms 3.9 levels):

- a white core: a box ±3.14 × ±0.3, blurred by 0.79 and saturating, 8 above the screen's edge;
- an azure glow around it, (0.16, 0.56, 1) × 0.66, with σ 3.42 across and 2.26 up and down;
- a spill on the seam, (0.76, 0.87, 1) × 0.35, with σ 6.62 and 0.78;
- a white shine on the lip, an ellipse 4.6 × 1.9.

`ShelfArt.light` draws it once for each size and scale, and the dot layer adds it with `plusL`. In dock2,
the spill above the seam adds (34, 47, 62) where the original added (31, 45, 62).

**Against the original, in the original's scene.** Most of these faults were found this way. The setup:

- The screenshot was shown behind dock2, a pixel to a point, on a window just under the Dock's level. Its
  back edge sat on dock2's, and the old icons were painted out above the back edge by interpolating the
  wallpaper between the gaps.
- dock2 matched the original's dock: tile 56, 16 tiles, a separator and three minimised windows, 1180 wide.
- The capture was reduced to 1× and compared column for column with the original.

The results:

- What the two floors reflect agrees within a level.
- In thirteen gaps between icons, the floor and lip differ by 4.4 levels on average.
- Under the window the front rows are still 7–9 darker: the original reflected about 0.23 there.
- The icons' reflections agree from 6 rows below the line down. In the rows nearest it dock2's are stronger:
  0.27 against 0.18–0.22, where the old icons' own shadows confound the original.

**The rest.**

- Under Snow Leopard the stage sets no platter glass.
- A layer of black at 1/255 covers the footprint, so the pointer between icons is still on the stage, as on
  the glass. Hover there showed the tile's label.
- Reduce Transparency hides the screen's reflection and keeps the icons'.
- Increase Contrast strokes the trapezoid in the light appearance's secondary label colour.
- Snow Leopard had one appearance, so the shelf, the lights and the lanes ignore the dark one.
- The style switches live in both directions; the platter's glass returns as a shape.
- Auto-hide slides the whole shelf away and leaves the screen's edge exactly the wallpaper.
- The shelf widens smoothly as tiles arrive.
- dock2 idles at about 4% CPU with the live mirror.
- All of this was seen in the debug build, at tile sizes 48, 56, 72 and 128.

## Observations

- One screenshot, at one tile size. How the shelf scales with the tile size is proportional until another
  size is measured. Leopard's four floor pictures are one picture at four sizes, so the floor at least
  scales without steps.
- **Open:** the floor's reflectance either side of the S. In the floor pictures the floor is translucent,
  0.56 to 0.77, and the dark grey is about 0.08 more transparent than the light at the same depth. Suppose
  the picture is drawn over the reflection, and 1 − alpha is carried from Generic RGB's gamma 1.8 into sRGB
  values. Then it reflects:
  - 0.35 at the back edge, against 0.32 measured;
  - at 0.6 of the way, 0.20 on the light side and 0.28 on the dark, against 0.24 measured by depth alone;
  - 0.20 at the front, against 0.19 measured.

  dock2 reflects by depth alone, so over bright content its light side likely reflects about 0.04 too much
  and its dark side 0.04 too little. Fitting ρ on each side of the S in the screenshot would settle it.
- The label, the badges and the number badges stay dock2's glass, since no screenshot shows Snow Leopard's.
  The panel stays Liquid Glass: it is dock2's own surface. Auto-hide's slide and the bounce are the glass's.
- The reflectance near the back edge rests on the stars, about 0.3. The fit over the wallpaper there was noisy.
  A screenshot over a light wallpaper would also say whether Leopard's mirror added to its tones or covered
  them; over a dark one the two are the same, which is why `plusL` reproduces it.
- **Open:** the icons' size. Snow Leopard's icons filled 86–96% of their tile; macOS 27's art fills 80%, so at
  the same tile size they read about a tenth smaller. They are drawn as the apps ship them.
- The mirror's colour space follows the window to a new screen through the relayout a display change
  brings, which is reasoned, not seen: only this Display P3 screen was tried.
- The first launch's preview stays the glass's.
