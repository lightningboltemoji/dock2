# Trees

> Change `1794451399` — 2026-11-11

## Goal

Plugins had no way to show data with a shape — a project's files, a mailbox's folders, an outline — short of
pushing a view per level, which hides the shape it is meant to show. They needed a list that holds items under
items, navigable from the keyboard, with the detail of the selected one beside it.

## Implementation

A tree is a list whose items have `children`, not a node of its own. Everything a list already does — sections,
filtering by dock2 or by the owner, scope, actions, empty and loading states, `selected`, and `preview` as the
detail pane — applies unchanged, and a `tree` node would have repeated every one of those fields and their
renderer. The SDK's `Item::children` and `Item::expanded` are the whole API.

What shows is Core's (`ListNode.rows(for:open:)` in `ListTree.swift`), tested there: the items under the open
ones, each with its depth, parent and whether it opens. With a query it is every match wherever it is, under the
items it stands in, opened for it and kept in the tree's order. A flat list ranks its matches best first, but a
tree's item means where it stands. `ListNode.best` selects the best match rather than the first row, which is
often an ancestor shown for context. An owner that filters gets its answer shown fully open. A match with nothing
matching under it shows no chevron while filtering. A flat list goes through `rows` too, as `filtered` always
had it, so the component has one path.

The open set belongs to `ListComponent`. An item's `expanded` is a hint taken as `selected` is, when it differs from
the last one given, so a plugin redrawing never undoes the user's opening and closing. `⌘N` in the demo shows a
redraw keeping both. An item named by `selected` inside closed items opens its ancestors. Clearing a tree's
filter hands the found item to the same pending mechanism, so the way to it opens and it is brought to the
middle. Without that, Esc after finding something lost it.

Rows stand in by `PanelMetrics.Row.indent` per level (20 pt, 16 compact) after one such column for the chevron,
so a child's chevron stands under its parent's icon, as in Finder's list view. Every row of a tree keeps the
column, so top-level leaves line up with top-level folders. A flat list keeps none. The chevron is
`chevron.right` at 11 pt semibold in the tertiary label colour, as Files' › is, turned −90° while open, and
animated over `rowFadeIn` when the same row changes state. Rows arriving and leaving already slide and fade, so
opening needs nothing more. A click anywhere before the icon toggles, which is wider than the glyph and easy to
hit.

→ and ← are the tree's only while the field is empty. With text typed they move the caret, and the tree is
already open around every match. This differs from the grid, which always takes them, and it means a query is
editable as text everywhere. ↩ on an item with children and no actions of its own toggles it. One with actions
runs them, as `docs` shows in the demo.

## Observations

- Seen in a VM, in the demo's A Tree and A Tree Beside a Preview: the layout at both densities, → ← open,
  close, go in and up, ↩ toggling a folder, typing `outline` showing both matches under their folders with the
  deeper one selected, Esc keeping it with its folders opened, and ⌘N keeping what is open and selected.
- Not done: children loaded as an item opens (an `expand` event the plugin answers), for trees too big or too
  remote to send whole; ⌥→ and ⌥← to open or close a whole branch, as Finder does.
- Indent and the chevron's size are judged from captures, not measured against Finder. Finder's 16 pt rows are
  denser than a view's 56 pt ones, so its numbers would not carry over directly.
