# The field's form: drop, gem, lava lamp

> Change `1792563748` — 2026-10-20

## Goal

The empty search field was an empty capsule, Spotlight's, and the user found it no fun. They wanted the
field to be something else until something is typed, and to turn into the field with an organic,
fluid motion as the first letter lands, the text starting in the middle and moving left as the field's left
end fills. Three ideas were prototyped as standalone probes: a drop of glass that pours out, an
icosahedron in a glass marble that unrolls, and a lava lamp of droplets that fling into line. The user
wanted all three, as a setting. On the gem, they asked that its opening not linger as a colourful strip, and
play like its closing in reverse.

## Implementation

**The probes.** A scratch app rendered each form in a copy of `GlassLayer` with the panel's recipe, at the
field's size, looping rest, fake typing, and a clear. They were recorded at 120 fps and tuned frame by frame.
The glass's SDF merge does the liquid work: overlapping elements melt into one outline with no seam, and
apart they neck and part. Three findings carried over. A front that leads the body by more than its own
radius breaks away and reads as broken, so fronts are tethered. A body squeezed narrower than it is tall
spikes, so it grows taller instead. Under the panel's blur (4.5 at half-resolution sampling) the gem was a
smear, so the marble has its own lens inputs and samples at full resolution.

**Simulated in Core, played by the window server.** The probes stepped springs on the main thread every
frame. That was rejected for the app: the first keystroke is exactly when search does its work on the main
thread, so the pour would stutter at the one moment it matters. CASpringAnimation per element was rejected
too: it cannot express the coupling (tethers, fronts that thin as the body arrives, faces whose glass
follows them) that made the probes read as liquid. Instead `FieldMotion` (Dock2Core, pure and tested) is
the probes' simulation made deterministic. `FormDriver` samples it ahead at every change and hands the
samples to Core Animation as keyframes (`Keyframes`, played by `GlassLayer.play`, `PanelShadow.play`,
`GemFaces.play` and `FieldView.play`).

**Composing with the panel's motion.** The first letter nearly always brings results, so the pour and the
panel's growth must run together. `FieldMotion` keeps the panel's intended body as a tween (the same
duration and Bézier `PanelController` uses) and expresses each form as a deformation relative to it. The
drop's fronts are anchored to the band's ends, the lumps to its middle, and the body's ends are
differences from the band's. So a widening (Files) or a growth carries the deformation along rather than
counting it twice. The clip and the rows' mask follow the deformed body. The rows were first seen
sticking out past a still-narrow glass for about 150 ms, so `rowsMask` (a container around the existing
`reveal`) is kept to the body's width. Emptied with results showing, the bar first collapsed while the
panel was still tall, a brief pillar. Now the rest waits until the target tween ends (`pendingRest`).

**Looping the rest.** The rest moves forever, so it cannot be sampled to a settled end. Every driver of
rest motion is a whole number of cycles in 24 s: the drop's breathing periods are 2, 2.4, 3 and 4 s, the
lava's anchors are integer multiples of 2π/24, and the gem tumbles three and two turns about its two axes.
The gem's tumble is a pure function of time; it no longer slows while poured, which kept the loop exact.
After a change, the driver samples 3 s, enough for a lava droplet's spring transient to die, then plays a
cached 24 s loop at 20 Hz with `timeOffset` set to the media time. A change in the loop region rebuilds
the state from `FieldMotion.resting(at:)`, which warms a rest for 4 s. The tests check that this equals
coming to rest from a pour, and that the loop repeats.

**The gem.** Its faces now fade as they fly (alpha to 0 once 55% of the way, over 0.18 s) and start from the
middle out over 0.12 s, the closing's stagger. The strip is never seen whole. Its glass pours out on its own
springs rather than following the faces, which had held the panel narrow for about half a second. The marble's
lens (blur 0.35, refraction −28 over 14 pt) animates back to the panel's inputs over the slide as it
pours, and returns as it rests. The gem style samples at full resolution throughout.

**The field.** The text field never moves: transforms and opacity are played on its layer, the
magnifier's and the spinner's as keyframes alone, ending where AppKit put them. A clear draws the old text
into a ghost layer, which the text's track draws into the middle as it fades. At rest the text field's track
hides its caret and placeholder. `FieldView.onEdit` reports every change before `onTextChange`, with
`cleared` for one set rather than typed. `hitArea` keeps the invisible field around a resting form from
taking clicks.

**Setting.** `Settings.fieldForm`, default Drop, a picker at the top of Settings → Search; it applies from
the next showing. The form is the capsule under Reduce Motion, Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast, or
without the private glass. Those settings need what the forms are made of to be different (no motion, a
solid fill, a stroke), and a sharp union of the shapes would not match the glass's smooth merge.

**Verified.** `make test` passes, with `FieldFormTests` added: each form settles exactly into the field
with everything else inside it; rests small and central; repeats each period; comes to rest as its loop
shows; does not jump at an event; keeps the text behind the glass's left edge; grows with the panel;
collapses only after the panel shrinks back; and the drop splashes. Live, on the debug build in dark mode,
recorded at 120 fps by revealing the dock and posting keys to dock2's process alone: each form at rest
over a split dark and white backdrop; the pour composing with results' growth; Esc's clear and the ghost;
backspacing to empty; and the entrance.

## Observations

- Not seen live: light mode; the capsule under `DOCK2_REDUCE_MOTION` or the other two display settings;
  Files or a view opened from a resting form; a display option changed while a form plays, which keeps the
  form until the next showing.
- A splash is hidden inside the panel's glass whenever results show above the field, which is nearly
  always. The drop's splashes only show over the field alone.
- The bake's cost was not measured. It is a few thousand values per change and tens of thousands for a
  rest loop, built once per showing.
- The shadow follows the body only. Under a drop's front or a lava droplet the shadow is not cut away, so
  the glass refracts a little of it.
- Posted to a process, a key event needs its characters set explicitly (Esc `0x1b`, delete `0x7f`): with
  only a virtual key code, Esc and delete never reached the field.
