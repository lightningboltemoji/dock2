# The tile numbers: at once, on the glass's band, out of the dot

> Change `1793854820` — 2026-11-04

## Goal

Three notes on `1793854001`, from using it. A flat 0.4 s wait made every hold feel slow to solve a problem only ⌘space has.
The digits were hard to read at the platter's edge. And a fade is a dull way for a number to arrive when there is a dot
right there for it to come out of.

## Implementation

**The block.** The wait is gone: ⌘ shows the numbers at once, unless dock2 took the keyboard or a key went down in the last
0.3 s. Asked for ⌘space alone, but ⌘space is one case of it, since space is a key. The same rule keeps a chord from
blinking them, and without it a key would hide them and they would be back in the same turn. Taking the keyboard starts
the block rather than the summon shortcut does, so Clipboard History's and a plugin's shortcuts have it without any
plumbing; a pointer reveal with ⌘ down has it too, unasked and unnoticeable.

That removed the state. `updateNumbers` is now one expression, called whenever an input changes, and `numberDelay`'s work
item, the `keyPressed` argument and `hideNumbers` went with it. A `flagsChanged` passes its own flags: a fresh ⌘ now has
to answer inside the event, with no later look to catch a read that lagged, and `NSEvent.modifierFlags` was not shown to
be current there. Time since the last key could have come from `CGEventSource.secondsSinceLastEventType`, as the dwell's
does, with no block to keep; `dock2 summon` presses no key, so no run could have tested it.

**The place.** The first try centred the digit between the platter's foot and the icon's art, which looked right on a dark
terminal and was wrong. Over a busier window the glass showed why: its rim refracts a capsule of band along the foot. A
pixel column between tiles, over navy, reads 62 above it and 155 on it, flat from 5 to 13 pt up, with its edges centred at
about 4 and 14. At the dot's height, 6, a digit spanned 2 to 10 and the band's lower edge cut
it: that was the complaint. Centred in the gap, at 11.75, it spanned 8 to 16, the upper edge. At 9, an 11 pt digit measures
5.0 to 13.0 on every tile. It is the one height that works, and 3 pt up, not the 5.75 the gap gave. Below tile 48 the
art leaves less room, so the number stands as high as a digit clears the art, and never below the dot.

**The rise.** A number's layer keeps its destination, its place or the dot, and `raise` and `lower` animate to it from the
presentation layer, so a tap that changes its mind turns round from where the digit is; eight taps 30 ms apart ended right
in both states. A number that has been hidden has a stale place, so `raise` only trusts the presentation layer with an
animation in flight. At 0.3 of its size the digit starts shorter than the dot, inside it, and the dot's 0.08 s fade reads
as one turning into the other in the frames; it fades as it lands, late, or it would be gone before it arrived.

0.55 damping was too timid, with a 0.7 pt overshoot on an 8 pt glyph, so 0.5. With the rise down to 3 pt most of the spring
is in the scale. Under Reduce Motion `raise` and `lower` leave the position and scale as they are, a cross-fade in place,
which was filmed; `1793854001` left that untried.

Measured through `debug hold`, polling `debug state`: ⌘ through the summon, 0.30 s; let go at 0.2 s, never; a fresh ⌘, on
the first poll; ⌘ pressed 0.15 s into the block, 0.14 s later; a chord, gone and back at 0.32 s; ⌘⇧ gone, and back on the
first poll; no keyboard, none; dismissed in the block, none. Filmed at 60 frames a second in light at 128. Looked at: dark
at 128 and a relayout to 96, numbers on, in the same places; Snow Leopard at 56, where it only grows.

## Observations

- A real ⌘ was not exercised here either, for the same reason as before. The fresh press is the case that changed: if it
  fails, `flagsChanged` is where to look.
- No delay means a ⌘ chord inside dock2 starts the numbers rising before its key lands, and the key sends them back: ⌘K
  blinks them partway. Unmeasured on a real hand, and the block cannot help, since it starts with the key. Typing just
  before the chord does hold it off.
- The band's 5 to 13 was measured at 128 and read by eye at 48. Whether it scales past 128, as `9 * magnification` assumes,
  is untested, as is the case below 48. Past 128 the digit outgrows the band anyway.
- The band's contrast depends on the backdrop: over one dark all the way down there is no band, and in the light appearance
  the digit is back to black on dark.
- The spring was tuned at 128 only. Below 48 the rise is under a point and only the scale shows.
- The rise was filmed in the light appearance only: the dark one's plus-lighter digit was seen at rest, not moving.
- The digit height in `DockMetrics`, 0.72 of the size, is the system font's: a different font would need its own.
